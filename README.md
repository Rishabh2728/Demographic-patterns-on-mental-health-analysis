Mental health has increasingly become a major focus of global public health, particularly
in the wake of the COVID-19 pandemic. The pandemic brought with it not only a
physical health crisis but also a significant psychological toll due to social isolation,
uncertainty, economic instability, and disruptions to daily life. These challenges led to a
notable rise in mental health symptoms such as anxiety and depression across various
segments of the population.

This project, titled “Mental Health Trends Analysis,” aims to explore and analyse
patterns in mental health indicators using real-world survey data collected from the
United States. The dataset includes mental health symptoms segmented by
demographic subgroups (such as age and gender), geographic regions (states), and time
frames that align with specific public health response phases during the pandemic.
Using the powerful Python libraries Pandas, NumPy, Matplotlib, and Seaborn, this
project performs data cleaning, statistical aggregation, and visualization to uncover
trends, disparities, and key patterns. The objectives include understanding which
subgroups are most affected, how mental health metrics have changed over time, how
different states compare, and how reliable the reported data is through confidence
intervals and quartile analysis.

By conducting this analysis, the project aims to provide insights that can help public
health professionals, policymakers, and mental health advocates better understand the
scope and variation of mental health issues during challenging periods. Such insights are
crucial for designing effective interventions, allocating resources, and crafting datadriven awareness campaigns.
